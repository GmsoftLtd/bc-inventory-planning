Frequently asked questions

Formulas and methodology

What formula does safety stock use?

SS = Z × √( LT × σD² + D² × σLT² )

Where Z is the inverse normal at your service level, LT the lead time in days, σD the standard deviation of daily demand, D the average daily demand and σLT the standard deviation of lead time.

It covers variability in both demand and lead time. A simpler demand-only formula understates the buffer for items whose supplier is unreliable.

Where do the Z-scores come from?

A continuous inverse-normal approximation (Acklam), not a lookup table. A 94.9% service level gets a 94.9% buffer rather than being rounded down to the nearest tabulated bucket.

How is the reorder point calculated?

ROP = (average daily demand × lead time) + safety stock

Capped at the item's Maximum Inventory when one is set. The safety stock used is the freshly calculated one, not the value stored on the item.

How is EOQ calculated?

EOQ = √( 2 × D × S / H )

The Wilson formula, where D is annual demand, S the ordering cost, and H the annual holding cost per unit — unit cost multiplied by your holding rate. The result is capped at a configurable number of months of demand and reconciled with the item's Minimum Order Quantity, Maximum Order Quantity and Order Multiple.

How are demand patterns classified?

By the Syntetos–Boylan scheme, using ADI (how often demand occurs) and CV² (how variable the quantities are):

PatternADICV²Recommended policy
Smooth< 1.32< 0.49Fixed Reorder Qty.
Erratic< 1.32≥ 0.49Fixed Reorder Qty.
Intermittent≥ 1.32< 0.49Lot-for-Lot
Lumpy≥ 1.32≥ 0.49Lot-for-Lot

Two overrides come first: an item with an Expiration Calculation, or one that already has a Maximum Inventory, is steered to Maximum Qty. instead. Make-to-order items are recommended Order.

Both thresholds are configurable.

What counts as demand?

Sales, netted against same-day returns, measured across every calendar day in the history window. Days with no demand count toward the average, which is what makes the daily rate comparable with a lead time expressed in calendar days.

With Count Consumption as Demand enabled, production and assembly consumption count too.

How is lead time determined?

  1. Purchase receipt history — mean and standard deviation of order-to-receipt days from the trailing two years. The only source that also yields variability.
  2. The item's Lead Time Calculation — also used for produced and assembled items.
  3. The fallback you set in setup.

The source used is recorded on every log entry. Safety stock and reorder point share this resolution, so they cannot disagree.

Is this forecasting or AI?

No. It is descriptive statistics over posted history. That is deliberate: every number can be traced back to transactions you can open and check. The app tells you when history is a poor guide to the present — see the trend warning — rather than predicting around it.

Behaviour

Will it overwrite values my planners maintain?

Only if you let it. Tick Exclude from Inventory Planning on those items and no calculator, bulk run or scheduled job will touch them. You can also turn off any individual Write … to Item switch to calculate and log without writing at all.

Does it change my reordering policies?

Only with two separate opt-ins. Auto-Update Item Reordering Policy is off by default; recommendations are logged either way. And even when enabled, it never sets a policy of Order automatically.

Separately, Set Policy to Fixed Reorder Qty. when None gives a policy to items that have none, so a calculated reorder point does something. It is suppressed for any item whose advice recommends a different policy.

What happens if I uninstall the app?

The values it calculated stay on your items, because they live in standard Business Central fields. You lose the setup, the calculation log and the per-item controls.

Does it work per location?

No. It calculates at item level. When an item has Stockkeeping Units, standard planning reads the SKU values at a location rather than the item card — and the app says so explicitly in the calculation note, rather than leaving you to discover it.

Does it work in a demo or evaluation company?

Yes. History windows anchor to the work date, not the system date, so a company whose demo data sits in the past still produces real results. In production the two are the same.

Can I run different calculators on different schedules?

Yes. Create several Job Queue entries with different Parameter Strings — see Running the calculators.

Data and privacy

Does my data leave my tenant?

No. The app reads item ledger entries, purchase receipt lines and item master data, and writes to standard item planning fields plus its own setup and log tables. All of it stays in your environment.

What telemetry does the app collect?

Diagnostic events on the standard Business Central telemetry channel, sent to the publisher's Application Insights resource:

EventRaised whenData
GSO0001A scheduled run completesMode, item count
GSO0002An interactive bulk run completesCalculation type, item count
GSO0003The dynamic provider computes valuesEvent count only

These carry counts and codes only — never item numbers, quantities, costs, customer names or any other business data. Telemetry also flows to your own environment's Application Insights resource if you have configured one. See the privacy statement.
