Planning parameters calculated from history you already have

Safety stock, reorder point, EOQ and a recommended reordering policy, computed from your posted item ledger entries and written to the standard Business Central fields. Free and open source.

The problem it solves

Business Central's planning engine is capable and correct. It also does exactly what your item cards tell it to — and on most systems that means Safety Stock Quantity is empty, Reorder Point was typed in at go-live by somebody estimating, and Reorder Quantity is a round number nobody has revisited since.

The engine executes those guesses faithfully. The business absorbs the result as stockouts on the items that matter and dead stock on the ones that don't.

Why this one

It writes standard fields

There is no parallel data model. Standard planning, requisition worksheets and MRP consume the results immediately with nothing to reconfigure — and if you ever uninstall the app, the values stay on your items.

It shows its working

Every calculation records the demand statistics behind it, the lead time and where that lead time came from, the service level and Z-score, the costs, the previous value and the reasoning. When somebody asks why an item's reorder point is what it is, the answer is one click away — months later.

It tells you when to doubt it

Very few planning tools admit their own limits. This one warns you when recent demand has diverged from the historical window, when Stockkeeping Units mean the planning engine reads a different value than the item card, and when an item's demand is too intermittent for the underlying statistics to be trusted.

You review before you commit

The Planning Worksheet shows current versus proposed values side by side for any filtered set of items, tells you how many lines change the reorder point by more than 25%, and applies only the lines you tick. Applying re-runs the calculators through their validated, logged path — nothing is blind-copied from the preview.

How your team uses it

  • One item — from the Item Card, while you are looking at it
  • A category or a vendor's range — from the Item List, on your current filter or selection
  • A reviewed rollout — through the Planning Worksheet
  • Unattended — a nightly Job Queue entry, created for you from the setup page
  • At planning time — optionally, values computed live during a planning run rather than read from the item

What it does not do

It calculates at item level. Per-location planning through Stockkeeping Units, ABC-driven service levels and exception workbenches are outside its scope — and the app tells you, in the calculation note, when an item has SKUs so you are never left wondering why a value "isn't working".

It is descriptive statistics over posted history, not forecasting and not machine learning. That is a deliberate choice: every number it produces can be traced back to transactions you can open.
